# Causal Magic System

This document imports the central Augrant principle:

> Magic and ordinary physics share one causal world. Powerful magic is allowed. Unaccounted magic is not.

## Casting model

A magical effect may require:
- learned method;
- skill;
- concentration;
- energy;
- components;
- geometry;
- words, gestures, symbols, tools, or devices;
- source access;
- target access;
- range;
- preparation;
- environmental conditions.

A cast is an attempt to cause a real state change, not a narrative exemption from physics.

## Transfer model

The first player-facing magical tradition centers on transfer.

A valid transfer identifies:

1. **Quantity** — what is being moved or redistributed?
2. **Source** — where does it come from?
3. **Coupling** — how can the caster access it?
4. **Capacity** — how much can the caster/control structure handle?
5. **Retention** — is it transferred immediately or briefly stored?
6. **Direction** — does the quantity have a vector?
7. **Receiver** — where can it go?
8. **Compatibility** — can that receiver meaningfully accept it?
9. **Loss** — how much dissipates or becomes unusable?
10. **Consequence** — what happens physically afterward?

## Starting domain: momentum

Early techniques manipulate small amounts of momentum.

Important consequences:
- moving targets are often easier to disrupt than stable ones;
- timing against commitment can matter more than raw force;
- the caster cannot simply erase unlimited momentum;
- redirecting momentum can endanger the caster or environment;
- stored momentum decays;
- large masses require much greater capacity;
- poor vector control can worsen the situation.

## Later domains

Possible advanced study may include:
- heat;
- vibration/sound;
- light;
- pressure;
- electrical charge;
- selected chemical or biological processes;
- spatial relationships;
- other carefully defined magical quantities.

Cross-domain conversion is **not automatically permitted**.

Discovering that a proposed conversion does not work is a valid magical result.

## Failure modes

A failed magical attempt may be:
- null;
- weak;
- misdirected;
- partially coupled;
- interrupted;
- unusually expensive;
- unstable;
- damaging to the focus/tool;
- dangerous to the caster;
- dangerous to the receiver.

Failure should teach something when circumstances are informative.

## Information limits

Magic does not automatically know facts.

A sensing technique can only reveal information its actual signal/mechanism can obtain.

## Magic and injuries

Healing must operate on actual physiological problems.

"Restore 10 HP" may represent regained fighting capacity, but curing internal bleeding, infection, nerve damage, poison, or organ injury requires an effect capable of addressing those states.

## Magic and the world

Magic can affect:
- combat;
- travel;
- building;
- medicine;
- agriculture;
- transport;
- signaling;
- investigation;
- security;
- industry;
- religion;
- law.

The player is not restricted to combat use.


### Distributed networks

Causal magic may be composed into prepared multi-node networks.

A valid distributed network must still account for:
- local trigger or source events;
- prepared transmission or relay paths;
- branch identity and routing;
- receiver capacity;
- losses over distance and across junctions;
- failure behavior when links are cut, overloaded, or mis-keyed;
- power source and maintenance.

Distributed magic does not create free long-range information transfer. Greater reach or complexity requires a physically and magically supported path, and every added node creates additional failure modes.
