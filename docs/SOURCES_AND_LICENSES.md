## Augrant

Design reference repository:
- https://github.com/augrantd150/augrant

Relevant principles currently referenced:
- causal magic;
- transfer-magic model;
- skill progression through meaningful use/practice/instruction;
- persistent world state;
- high-skill combat through timing, positioning, balance, momentum, and observation.

The Black Tithe may simplify these principles but should document intentional divergence.

## League of Legends — Bard design research

Official champion reference:
- https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/champions/bard/

Bard is used only as a **design-analysis reference** for:
- roaming as a meaningful tradeoff;
- progression that encourages leaving the obvious path;
- abilities whose value depends on terrain/geometry;
- setup before a fight;
- traversal that can create counterplay;
- extremely high-impact utility where poor judgment can harm the user;
- a strange support/control identity that remains expressive after many games.

Do not copy:
- Bard's name as a player class;
- named abilities;
- lore;
- visual design;
- audio;
- character assets;
- exact numbers;
- proprietary text.

The production archetype is **The Wayfarer**, designed around Augrant-style causal transfer and dark-medieval world rules.

## Narrative-design research

The project's simulation-first GM rules were informed by the following public design material:

- Justin Alexander, **Don't Prep Plots** — prepare situations instead of predetermined event sequences:
  https://thealexandrian.net/creations/misc/prep-scenario.html
- Justin Alexander, **Three Clue Rule** — use redundant evidence to avoid mystery chokepoints:
  https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/1118/roleplaying-games/three-clue-rule
- Justin Alexander, **Node-Based Scenario Design** — structure scenarios as connected situations rather than a required linear path:
  https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/7949/roleplaying-games/node-based-scenario-design-part-1-the-plotted-approach
- Matt Brown / Maxis, GDC 2018, **Emergent Storytelling Techniques in The Sims** — balance authored structure with player-driven and emergent stories:
  https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1025112/
- Nick Popovich / Monomi Park, GDC 2017, **A Thousand Tiny Tales: Emergent Storytelling in Slime Rancher** — simple wants/needs and interacting systems can generate memorable stories:
  https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024296/
- Harvey Smith & Matthias Worch, GDC 2010, **What Happened Here? Environmental Storytelling** — let players infer history from environments and systemic reactions:
  https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1012696/
- Joshua Sawyer / Obsidian, GDC 2012, **Choice Architecture, Player Expression, and Narrative Design in Fallout: New Vegas** — structure interaction around player expression and agency:
  https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1016166/
- Paweł Sasko / CD Projekt RED, GDC 2023, **10 Key Quest Design Lessons from The Witcher 3 and Cyberpunk 2077** — quest design lessons on brevity, choice/consequence, emotional impact, and avoiding common narrative traps:
  https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1028897/
- Brandon Sanderson, BYU 2020 lecture, **Plot Part 1** — promises, progress, and payoff as a useful dramatic model:
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jrIogch5DBU

These are design references, not content sources to reproduce verbatim.
