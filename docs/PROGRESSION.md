# Progression System

## Principle

Character skill grows mainly through:
- meaningful use;
- deliberate practice;
- instruction;
- study;
- difficult work;
- combat drills;
- supervised magical practice;
- informative failure;
- lived experience.

Natural aptitude changes learning rate and ease, not the ultimate possibility of mastery.

## Visible skill state

Each important skill has:

- **Mastery score:** 0–100
- **Rank:** a readable label
- **Recent evidence:** why progress occurred
- **Technique unlocks:** capabilities that become reliably available
- **Current training focus:** optional deliberate practice target

Suggested ranks:

| Score | Rank |
|---:|---|
| 0–9 | Untrained |
| 10–24 | Novice |
| 25–39 | Practiced |
| 40–54 | Adept |
| 55–69 | Expert |
| 70–84 | Master |
| 85–94 | Grandmaster |
| 95–100 | Exceptional |

The top end should be extremely difficult to reach.

## Learning evidence

A meaningful event may generate learning evidence if it contains at least one of:
- real uncertainty;
- meaningful risk;
- a novel problem;
- correction from a teacher;
- accurate feedback;
- increased speed/precision requirement;
- a difficult opponent;
- unusual conditions;
- a failed model that reveals why it failed.

Repeated trivial actions generate sharply diminishing or zero progress.

## Progress notifications

After meaningful scenes, show progress explicitly.

Example:

`Footwork 18 -> 19 (Novice)`
Reason: maintained range during two committed attacks while exhausted.

`Momentum Transfer 27 -> 28 (Practiced)`
Reason: redirected a small moving mass into a prepared receiver without losing control.

## Player skill vs character skill

Both matter.

Character skill influences:
- consistency;
- precision;
- reaction window;
- energy efficiency;
- error tolerance;
- information quality;
- execution speed.

Player skill influences:
- what to attempt;
- when to attempt it;
- what information to seek;
- route selection;
- resource expenditure;
- combination of techniques;
- whether to fight at all.

A high-stat character used badly can die.

A skilled player can outperform raw numbers, but cannot make an untrained character perform impossible mastery.

## General advancement

There may still be broad milestones for durability, social standing, access, and narrative progression, but they must not replace domain-specific learning.

## Training ecology

Teachers, books, guilds, monasteries, battlefield veterans, occultists, surgeons, craftsmen, hunters, thieves, and institutions are progression resources.

Training has costs:
- money;
- favors;
- time;
- access;
- loyalty;
- danger;
- reputation;
- materials.

This makes advancement part of the world rather than a menu.
