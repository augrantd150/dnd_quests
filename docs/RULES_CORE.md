# Core Rules Contract

## Rules base

When using fifth-edition-derived mechanics, use **SRD 5.2.1** as the legal and mechanical reference.

This project is not required to preserve every standard 5E subsystem. The SRD provides a familiar chassis; The Black Tithe adds a more simulation-heavy solo layer.

## Resolution

Default uncertain action:

`d20 + relevant ability modifier + relevant training/proficiency modifiers`

Use Advantage/Disadvantage when the fiction strongly supports it.

Do not roll when:
- success is automatic;
- failure is impossible;
- the action is impossible;
- a deterministic physical result is already known.

## Information before commitment

Before a dangerous commitment, describe information the character would reasonably perceive.

Do not reveal hidden statistics by default.

A skilled character may receive more precise observations, such as:
- stance is trained;
- right knee is compromised;
- bow draw is heavy;
- attacker is overcommitted;
- floorboards will not support two armored bodies;
- a magical transfer has a visible anchor.

## Combat priorities

Combat is about:
1. range;
2. initiative and tempo;
3. facing;
4. footing and balance;
5. cover and line of sight;
6. reach;
7. stamina;
8. commitment and recovery;
9. injury;
10. morale and willingness to continue.

A weapon attack is not just a damage roll. Position and timing should affect what attacks are realistically available.

## Action economy

For the first playable text implementation, retain a familiar turn structure:
- movement;
- one main action;
- one quick/bonus-scale action when a technique grants it;
- one reaction when a trigger allows it;
- free speech and minor interaction when plausible.

Later real-time implementations may translate these into continuous timing windows.

## Hit Points and wounds

Hit Points remain a useful abstraction for near-misses, guard, stamina, luck, minor trauma, and fighting capacity.

However, important injuries are separate persistent state.

A **Wound** may be caused by:
- being reduced to 0 HP;
- critical or especially damaging attacks;
- specific damage mechanisms;
- failed risky physical actions;
- environmental harm.

Possible wound effects:
- bleeding;
- impaired movement;
- impaired hand/arm use;
- pain;
- shock;
- infection risk;
- reduced maximum HP until treated;
- scarring or permanent impairment in extreme cases.

## 0 HP and death

At 0 HP the character is not safely "down." Use the SRD 5.2.1 death-saving-throw framework as the starting point, with wounds and world causality layered on top.

Enemies do not automatically ignore a helpless target. Their behavior depends on goals, fear, training, cruelty, orders, time pressure, and perceived threats.

Death is canonical.

## Healing

Healing must distinguish:
- restored fighting capacity;
- stopped bleeding;
- pain control;
- tissue repair;
- infection treatment;
- bone repair;
- restoration of lost function.

Magic may accomplish extraordinary healing only if its mechanism supports the effect.

## Rest

Rest requires actual safety, time, shelter, hydration, food, and interruption handling.

Sleeping in a ditch during freezing rain is not mechanically equivalent to sleeping in a guarded bed.

## Retreat

Retreat is a first-class success state.

Encounters should support:
- disengagement;
- surrender;
- bargaining;
- hiding;
- barricading;
- route denial;
- buying time;
- abandoning loot;
- sacrificing equipment;
- luring pursuit.

## Enemy parity

Enemies use the same underlying physical and magical principles as the player.

They may have superior training, information, equipment, numbers, or techniques, but should not receive arbitrary immunity from the world's rules.

## Anti-cheese rule

If a tactic is physically valid, let it work.

If repeated use would logically cause adaptation, let enemies learn.

Do not invalidate clever play merely because it is strong.
